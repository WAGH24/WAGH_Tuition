Solved ExamplesEuclid’s postulates and constructions

📋 Given
If \(A,B,C\) are collinear and \(B\) lies between \(A\) and \(C\), prove that \(AB+BC=AC\).
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Use Euclid’s idea of adding adjacent segments on the same straight line.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. The segment \(AC\) consists exactly of the two non-overlapping segments \(AB\) and \(BC\).
  2. Lengths are additive for adjacent segments.
  3. Therefore \(AB+BC=AC\).
✅ Final Answer
\(\boxed{AB+BC=AC}\).
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
\(\boxed{AB+BC=AC}\).
📋 Given
Construct an equilateral triangle on a given line segment \(AB\) using Euclid’s postulates.
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Use Postulate 3: a circle can be drawn with any centre and radius; use equality of radii and common notions.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Draw a circle with centre \(A\) and radius \(AB\).
  2. Draw a circle with centre \(B\) and radius \(BA\).
  3. Let the circles meet at \(C\). Join \(AC\) and \(BC\).
  4. Since \(AC=AB\) and \(BC=BA\), we have \(AB=BC=CA\).
✅ Final Answer
Triangle \(ABC\) is equilateral.

Exercise 5.1Axioms, definitions and proofs

📋 Given
Which of the following statements are true and which are false? Give reasons.
(i) Only one line can pass through a single point.
(ii) There are infinitely many lines which pass through two distinct points.
(iii) A terminated line can be produced indefinitely on both sides.
(iv) If two circles are equal, then their radii are equal.
(v) In Fig. 5.9, if \(AB=PQ\) and \(PQ=XY\), then \(AB=XY\).
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Apply Euclid’s postulates, definitions and Common Notion 1: things equal to the same thing are equal to one another.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. (i) False. Infinitely many lines can pass through one point.
  2. (ii) False. Exactly one line passes through two distinct points.
  3. (iii) True. This is Euclid’s second postulate.
  4. (iv) True. Equal circles have equal radii by definition.
  5. (v) True. Since \(AB=PQ\) and \(PQ=XY\), Common Notion 1 gives \(AB=XY\).
✅ Final Answer
False, False, True, True, True.
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
ખોટું, ખોટું, સાચું, સાચું, સાચું.
📋 Given
Give definitions of: (i) parallel lines, (ii) perpendicular lines, (iii) line segment, (iv) radius of a circle, (v) square. Are there undefined terms that must be used?
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Definitions in geometry are built from previously accepted primitive or undefined terms.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. (i) Parallel lines: coplanar lines that never meet however far they are produced.
  2. (ii) Perpendicular lines: lines meeting to form a right angle.
  3. (iii) Line segment: the part of a line between two endpoints.
  4. (iv) Radius: a segment joining the centre of a circle to any point on the circle.
  5. (v) Square: a quadrilateral with four equal sides and four right angles.
  6. The definitions use undefined notions such as point, line, plane, distance and angle.
✅ Final Answer
The definitions are as listed; primitive terms such as point, line and plane are accepted without formal definition.
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
વ્યાખ્યાઓ ઉપર મુજબ છે; બિંદુ, રેખા અને સમતલ જેવા મૂળભૂત પદો અપરિભાષિત સ્વીકારવામાં આવે છે.
📋 Given
Consider the two postulates:
(i) Given any two distinct points \(A\) and \(B\), there exists a third point \(C\) lying between them.
(ii) There exist at least three points that are not on the same line.
Do these postulates contain undefined terms? Are they consistent? Do they follow from Euclid’s postulates?
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Analyse the language, construct a model, and distinguish an independent axiom from a consequence.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Undefined terms include point, line, “lies between”, and “on the same line”.
  2. The postulates are consistent: ordinary Euclidean geometry provides a model satisfying both.
  3. Postulate (i) follows from the ability to draw a segment and the assumed continuity of a line, but is not explicitly one of Euclid’s five postulates.
  4. Postulate (ii) is an additional incidence assumption ensuring that geometry is not restricted to one line; it also does not directly follow from the five postulates alone.
✅ Final Answer
They use undefined terms, are mutually consistent, and are treated as additional assumptions rather than direct consequences of Euclid’s five postulates.
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
તેમાં અપરિભાષિત પદો છે, બંને સુસંગત છે અને વધારાની સ્વીકાર્યતાઓ છે.
📋 Given
If a point \(C\) lies between two points \(A\) and \(B\) such that \(AC=BC\), prove that \(AC=\frac12 AB\). Explain by drawing the figure.
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Segment addition and substitution.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Because \(C\) lies between \(A\) and \(B\), \(AB=AC+CB\).
  2. Given \(AC=BC\), replace \(CB\) by \(AC\).
  3. Then \(AB=AC+AC=2AC\).
  4. Dividing by 2 gives \(AC=\frac12 AB\).
✅ Final Answer
\(\boxed{AC=BC=\frac12 AB}\).
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
\(\boxed{AC=BC=\frac12 AB}\).
📋 Given
In Question 4, point C is called a midpoint of line segment AB. Prove that every line segment has one and only one midpoint.
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Use proof by contradiction and Euclid’s Common Notion: the whole is greater than the part.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Assume a side \(AB\) has two distinct midpoints \(C\) and \(D\).
  2. Then \(AC=CB\) and \(AD=DB\).
  3. If \(C\) and \(D\) are distinct, one lies between the other and an endpoint; suppose \(C\) lies between \(A\) and \(D\).
  4. Then \(AD=AC+CD>AC\), but the midpoint equalities force both to be half of \(AB\), a contradiction.
  5. Therefore the midpoint is unique. The argument applies to every side.
✅ Final Answer
Each side has exactly one midpoint.
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
દરેક બાજુનું માત્ર એક જ મધ્યબિંદુ હોય છે.
📋 Given
In Fig. 5.10, if \(AC=BD\), prove that \(AB=CD\), where the points lie in the order \(A-B-C-D\).
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
Subtract equal common lengths from equal lengths.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Because the points are in order, \(AC=AB+BC\).
  2. Also, \(BD=BC+CD\).
  3. Given \(AC=BD\), so \(AB+BC=BC+CD\).
  4. Subtract \(BC\) from both sides.
  5. Hence \(AB=CD\).
✅ Final Answer
\(\boxed{AB=CD}\).
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
\(\boxed{AB=CD}\).
📋 Given
Why is Axiom 5, “The whole is greater than the part”, considered a universal truth?
🎯 To Find
Complete the required proof or calculation.
📘 Formula / Concept Used
A universal truth applies to magnitudes and objects in every context, not only to geometric figures.
✏️ Step-by-Step Solution
  1. Whenever a proper part is taken from an object or magnitude, the part contains less than the complete object.
  2. The statement applies to lengths, areas, volumes, numbers, sets and physical objects.
  3. It does not depend on a special geometric construction, so it is accepted as a universal common notion.
✅ Final Answer
It is universal because every proper part is smaller than its whole in all mathematical and everyday contexts.
🌐 Gujarati Translation — Final Answer
કોઈપણ યોગ્ય ભાગ તેના સંપૂર્ણ કરતાં નાનો હોય છે, તેથી આ સર્વવ્યાપી સત્ય છે.
